A second model for comparison. Right now you have one model and no baseline ML comparison. Every reviewer will ask "why XGBoost specifically, and does it beat simpler alternatives?" You need Random Forest at minimum, trained on the same folds. One extra model, same pipeline, two days of work. Without it the paper feels incomplete.
The permutation test. Your regime finding is interesting but a reviewer will ask whether the overall result could be due to chance. Run the labels shuffled 500 times and show your real AUC beats 95%+ of random runs. This is the difference between "our model works" and "we can prove it isn't noise."
A literature review. You have no citations yet except Lo (2004). You need 15-25 references covering ML in stock markets, technical analysis literature, emerging/frontier market studies, and sentiment in finance. This is probably two to three weeks of reading and writing, and it is non-negotiable for any journal.
The sentiment pipeline. Right now your feature set is purely technical — RSI, MACD, volume, returns. You designed the sentiment pipeline, you know it adds novelty. Without it the paper's contribution is "XGBoost on technical features in NEPSE" which is thin. With sentiment it becomes "multi-source sentiment-augmented ML signals in a frontier market" which is publishable at a decent venue.
A proper introduction and conclusion. You have results but no framing of why this matters, what gap you fill, or what practitioners and researchers should take from it.
